# Curriculum review — 20 October 2026

All thirty lessons were read for objective–exercise–rubric alignment, prerequisite order, clarity, manageable scope, originality of wording and useful repetition. This is an editorial review, not evidence that the curriculum has already improved learner outcomes. The sources and their access limitations remain in [CURRICULUM.md](CURRICULUM.md).

Fifteen lessons received targeted edits; their stable IDs remain unchanged and their content versions were incremented. No lessons were added. The main corrections were premature rhyme demands, an incorrect numbered cross-reference, contrived examples, repeated bereavement imagery, and tasks that asked for too much new writing in five minutes.

## Repetition that is useful, and repetition that was weakened

- Sensory detail → emotional action → subtext revisits evidence while changing the inference task. Repeated grief imagery was reduced to broaden expressive range.
- Speech → natural rhyme → meaningful rhyme revision now follows the actual teaching order.
- Character → obstacle → setup/payoff → reversal → timing → character-driven humour reuses saved material rather than demanding disconnected prompts.
- Verse/chorus → title-hook context → bridge perspective adds a different structural job each time.
- Stress → pulse → phrasing → emphasis → one-hearing revision asks a different question on each pass. None can be fully assessed as a musical performance through text alone.
- Local revision (lesson 20), intention-led integration (29), and the miniature capstone (30) overlap intentionally. Their scopes and rubrics distinguish editing two lines, coordinating multiple choices, and evaluating an ending.

## Originality check and limits

All thirty exercise strings are distinct. A normalised comparison of the revised examples and exercises against the three previously fetched source snapshots (Pattison’s article, Stolpe’s article, and Penguin’s Fry overview) found no matching twelve-word sequence. This is a limited check against the consulted materials, **not comprehensive plagiarism clearance**. Common techniques, short phrases, rhetorical patterns and verse forms are not claimed as inventions. No substantive Sheila Davis material was consulted; there are no invented Davis citations.

## Assessment and remaining validation

The tutor receives each objective, exercise, rubric and retrieval objectives. It should cite a detail, explain an effect and offer at most two next steps. It must tolerate dialect, deliberate irregularity and subjective taste. Numerical scores do not control access or advancement. Completion/XP still records engagement, not mastery; minimum text/reflection lengths cannot establish quality.

The first learning profile selects interests, form, experience and a gentle goal. These frame prompts and tutor context; they do not make every lesson genre-specific or replace prerequisites. Writing, reflection and revision remain possible offline, and earlier feedback remains available with its source draft identified.

The next educational validation should involve a working teacher and learners with different experience levels. Observe time-on-task for 17, 22, 29 and 30; ask learners to explain choices; then compare transfer to unfamiliar prompts. Do not substitute retention metrics or AI scores for evidence of learning. Those studies were not conducted in this cloud task.
