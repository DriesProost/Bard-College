# The Conservatory of the Lute — Music curriculum

**Approved educational plan — lessons 01–09 implemented in Phase 2 on 21 October 2026; lessons 10–36 and shared studios remain deferred.** Based on selective, documented consultation of all eight supplied books and direct checks of the key visual examples used in this plan. This is not a claim to have read every page, verified every score, heard companion audio, or demonstrated learner outcomes.

## Recommendation and progression

**36 composition lessons**, four stages of nine: (1) original gestures and rhythmic/pitch control, (2) harmony and accompaniment as choices, (3) melodic development and harmonisation, (4) form, colour and independent acoustic composition. Seven shared songwriting studios are separate from both faculty counts. The earlier 32-unit sketch is superseded; the remaining Music IDs are still proposals. The first nine Music IDs are now deployed and must also retain their identity; all 30 original Writing IDs remain preserved.

The added space makes compound metre, functional endings, transposition for the singer and bass movement explicit instead of hiding them in overloaded tasks. Memorising scales/chord shapes or playing an existing song never suffices for a composition lesson. Every unit below produces or revises an original gesture, phrase, accompaniment, section or rationale. Units 9, 18, 26 and 36 combine skills; unit 35 assembles existing material over several sessions.

Ask separately about guitar-playing proficiency, theoretical vocabulary and experience inventing music. Offer diagnostics that involve making and transforming a phrase, not a speed or repertoire test. An experienced guitarist may need early composition practice; a beginner can compose on one string or with two accessible shapes. Permit comfortable voicings, slower tempos and simplified textures. Required technique is the minimum needed to express the current idea; refer persistent discomfort to an appropriate teacher rather than demand repetition through pain.

## Teaching and assessment

The goal is independent creative decisions, not lesson completion. A normal session is one minute noticing an example, three minutes making or revising, and one minute explaining the choice. A unit can require several sessions. Optional 15–30-minute practice and multi-session projects are labelled honestly; completing a whole song is never a five-minute promise. The timing and sequence are our instructional design, not the books' programme reproduced in miniature. Kachulis recommends at least half an hour for a source lesson.

Use four evidence states: introduced; demonstrated with support; independently demonstrated; retained/transferred. Keep “insufficient evidence” separate from failure. A completed exercise/XP records participation. Recommend readiness after a relevant attempt and explained revision; confirm retention with a delayed unfamiliar prompt. Learners can practise or attempt a diagnostic at any level; a bypass is not mastery. No scalar AI score controls access.

Review after approximately 1, 3, 7 and 14 days, adapting to errors and learner choice. These intervals are our unvalidated starting heuristic. Each unit retrieves its listed prerequisite skills; every stage challenge combines at least two skills. Reuse saved work for focused revision, then test transfer with a different subject or musical constraint. A learner may reasonably retain an irregularity and explain it.

The GPT-6 Luna tutor receives the relevant original explanation, objective, task, criterion IDs, representation, intention, selected earlier work and evidence state. It cites a specific observation, explains its likely effect, offers one or two useful revisions and one question. It teaches without replacing the student's work, avoids excessive praise and acknowledges taste, dialect and cultural context. Offline self-assessment and revision use the same criteria. Human review remains appropriate for ambiguous judgments and stage portfolios.

Source codes below resolve to precise consulted sections in [LIBRARY_CATALOGUE.md](LIBRARY_CATALOGUE.md). **T** means prose consulted, **V** means the specified visual claim checked, **D** means our instructional design, and **U** means unverified/excluded from evidentiary claims. A source supports the named principle, not authorship or endorsement of our example, task, schedule or assessment. All examples below are original; “weaker” means weaker evidence for that task, not artistically forbidden.

## Tab-first practical language

Use **tablature, chord diagrams, string/fret positions, chord symbols and counted rhythm** as primary representations. Standard notation is optional and never a readiness or assessment gate. Explain note names and scale degrees beside frets so that theory is usable without staff reading. Each example states tuning, capo, string orientation, meter, count, durations/rests and sounding-versus-shape chord names. Guitar staff notation conventionally sounds an octave below its written treble pitch; tab locations and actual sounding pitches must stay consistent.

ASCII tab reads high e at the top and low E at the bottom. Chord-shape strings are listed low E→A→D→G→B→high e, with x muted and 0 open. A beginner example in standard tuning, no capo, 4/4:

```text
count:  1    2    3    4
e|------0----1----3----r---|
B|-----------------------|
G|-----------------------|
D|-----------------------|
A|-----------------------|
E|-----------------------|
length: q    q    q    q-rest
```

Here `q` means one quarter-note beat and `r` a rest. In actual app assets the counted slots must align visibly; equal spacing by itself is not a duration notation. E4–F4–G4 are sounding pitches. An original C-shape label `x32010` yields C3–E3–G3–C4–E4; an Am shape `x02210` yields A2–E3–A3–C4–E4. These ordinary shapes are independently checked, not copied diagrams. The final application should render equivalent original chord diagrams with accessible text, not require the student to decode a long fret string unaided.

Before notation is fluent, use a scaffold of counted slots and only the fields needed for that task. For later work accept timed tab, named pitches/scale degrees or an equivalent clear plan. Text can show notes, durations, chord membership and reasoning; it cannot prove the performance, groove, intonation, vocal comfort or physical ease. Tutor wording must say “the written plan…” and identify missing evidence. A self-reported listening observation is a reflection, not a recording heard by AI.

## Stage 1: Gestures, pulse and pitch

### music-01 — An idea before a scale

**Objective / principle:** Turn an intention into two contrasting musical gestures. An intention suggests experiments, not a compulsory mood-to-chord formula.

**Original contrast:** A higher answering gesture can contrast with a low repeated note. Weaker evidence: Naming an emotion without making or describing a gesture supplies no compositional evidence.

**Five-minute task:** Choose one intention; play two short one-string gestures and record their frets plus one contrast.

**Readiness / retrieval:** Entry. Review the named skills in the task; return to this sketch after a delay with one changed constraint.

**Assess:** Two identifiable alternatives; one choice linked to an intention. The learner documents one choice, a comparison and an intended effect.

**Source support:** R1, J1; entry design D. Task, comparison and rubric D.

### music-02 — Pulse and space

**Objective / principle:** Create an attack/rest pattern against a pulse. Silence is part of a composition.

**Original contrast:** Attacks on beats 1 and 3 with beats 2 and 4 silent define a repeatable choice. Weaker evidence: “Play some notes quickly” specifies neither pulse nor rests.

**Five-minute task:** Tap four beats; mark two attacks and a rest on a one-bar grid.

**Readiness / retrieval:** music-01. Review the named skills in the task; return to this sketch after a delay with one changed constraint.

**Assess:** The grid distinguishes attacks from silence; the learner can explain the repeated pattern. The learner documents one choice, a comparison and an intended effect.

**Source support:** J3, KH1. Task, comparison and rubric D.

### music-03 — Durations and subdivisions

**Objective / principle:** Control how long a note lasts, separately from when it begins.

**Original contrast:** Two eighths, one quarter, one quarter rest and one quarter fill four quarter-note beats. Weaker evidence: Five quarter notes labelled one 4/4 bar do not fit the stated metre.

**Five-minute task:** Write one 4/4 bar using quarter/eighth values and a rest; count it while playing a single pitch.

**Readiness / retrieval:** music-02. Review the named skills in the task; return to this sketch after a delay with one changed constraint.

**Assess:** Durations sum correctly; intended attacks and holds are unambiguous. The learner documents one choice, a comparison and an intended effect.

**Source support:** J3, KM1. Task, comparison and rubric D.

### music-04 — Three or four beats

**Objective / principle:** Change grouping rather than merely speed.

**Original contrast:** Accent beat 1 of each group of three, then each group of four. Weaker evidence: Playing the same accents faster does not demonstrate changed grouping.

**Five-minute task:** Create a short pattern in 3/4 and adapt it to 4/4; keep a comparable quarter-note pulse.

**Readiness / retrieval:** music-03. Review the named skills in the task; return to this sketch after a delay with one changed constraint.

**Assess:** Both bars are counted correctly; the learner identifies the changed grouping. The learner documents one choice, a comparison and an intended effect.

**Source support:** J3. Task, comparison and rubric D.

### music-05 — Six eighths, two beats

**Objective / principle:** Distinguish compound duple 6/8 from simple triple 3/4. Six equal subdivisions can be grouped differently.

**Original contrast:** A 3+3 accent grouping differs from 2+2+2 with the same six eighth-note slots. Weaker evidence: Calling every six-note bar 6/8 ignores the stated grouping.

**Five-minute task:** Create a six-attack pattern; count 6/8 as 1-la-li 2-la-li, then compare three groups of two.

**Readiness / retrieval:** music-04. Review the named skills in the task; return to this sketch after a delay with one changed constraint.

**Assess:** Labels both groupings; explains the difference without confusing tempo and metre. The learner documents one choice, a comparison and an intended effect.

**Source support:** R6c, J3; compound does not automatically mean swing. Task, comparison and rubric D.

### music-06 — Find and remember a pitch

**Objective / principle:** Connect string/fret locations with pitch names and an octave. Use playing/listening self-report without pretending text proves pitch matching.

**Original contrast:** Open e and B5 are the same sounding pitch in standard tuning; D2 is an octave lower. Weaker evidence: Matching fret numbers across different strings does not guarantee matching pitches.

**Five-minute task:** Find E on open high e and at B-string fret 5; add a lower E and label tuning.

**Readiness / retrieval:** music-01. Review the named skills in the task; return to this sketch after a delay with one changed constraint.

**Assess:** Locations agree with declared tuning; the learner distinguishes unison from octave. The learner documents one choice, a comparison and an intended effect.

**Source support:** KMref, KHbasics; independent fret arithmetic V. Task, comparison and rubric D.

### music-07 — Steps, leaps and a response

**Objective / principle:** Choose interval motion to contrast two short motifs.

**Original contrast:** On high e, frets 0–1–3 ascend by steps; 0–7 begins with a perfect fifth. Weaker evidence: Fret distance and scale-degree distance used interchangeably can misname an interval.

**Five-minute task:** Invent two three-note gestures, one mainly stepwise and one with a leap; name one interval.

**Readiness / retrieval:** music-03, music-06. Review the named skills in the task; return to this sketch after a delay with one changed constraint.

**Assess:** Correctly identifies the selected interval; makes an intentional alternative. The learner documents one choice, a comparison and an intended effect.

**Source support:** J1, KMref. Task, comparison and rubric D.

### music-08 — A scale as a palette

**Objective / principle:** Use a major collection to create a phrase, not to pass a speed test.

**Original contrast:** A C–E–D idea and a D–G–C answer can use the same collection differently. Weaker evidence: Ascending a memorised scale alone does not demonstrate answering an idea.

**Five-minute task:** Build C major with whole/half-step guidance; choose three notes and invent an answer.

**Readiness / retrieval:** music-07. Review the named skills in the task; return to this sketch after a delay with one changed constraint.

**Assess:** Chosen pitches fit the declared collection; the answer changes an identifiable feature. The learner documents one choice, a comparison and an intended effect.

**Source support:** J2; scale-construction scaffold D. Task, comparison and rubric D.

### music-09 — First miniature

**Objective / principle:** Combine rhythm, pitch choice and an intention in a two-bar original sketch.

**Original contrast:** Keep the first bar’s attacks but end the answer on a different chosen note. Weaker evidence: Adding an unrelated bar without a rationale gives weak continuity evidence.

**Five-minute task:** Reuse an earlier gesture, add one answering bar, and explain one retained and one changed feature.

**Readiness / retrieval:** music-04, music-08. Review the named skills in the task; return to this sketch after a delay with one changed constraint.

**Assess:** Both bars are documented; rhythm and pitch choices can be traced to the intention. The learner documents one choice, a comparison and an intended effect.

**Source support:** R1, J1; stage challenge D. Task, comparison and rubric D.

## Stage 2: Harmony and purposeful accompaniment

### music-10 — Chords have notes

**Objective / principle:** Build major/minor triads and use one in a tiny accompaniment.

**Original contrast:** C contains C–E–G; Am contains A–C–E. Both can support E differently. Weaker evidence: A familiar shape name without its notes does not demonstrate chord construction.

**Five-minute task:** Spell C and Am; choose a playable diagram and place it under one saved note.

**Readiness / retrieval:** music-08. Review the named skills in the task; return to this sketch after a delay with one changed constraint.

**Assess:** Spelling agrees with diagram; accompaniment choice relates to the note. The learner documents one choice, a comparison and an intended effect.

**Source support:** R2, KMref. Task, comparison and rubric D.

### music-11 — Chords in a key

**Objective / principle:** Derive I, IV and V in C rather than memorise a shape sequence.

**Original contrast:** C–F changes from C–E–G to F–A–C. Weaker evidence: Calling F an F-minor chord while spelling F–A–C is internally inconsistent.

**Five-minute task:** Build C, F and G from scale notes; choose a two-chord order for an original sketch.

**Readiness / retrieval:** music-10. Review the named skills in the task; return to this sketch after a delay with one changed constraint.

**Assess:** Correct notes/roman numerals; a deliberate ordered choice. The learner documents one choice, a comparison and an intended effect.

**Source support:** J13, R2. Task, comparison and rubric D.

### music-12 — An ending or a question

**Objective / principle:** Compare a major-key V–I arrival with an ending on V. Cadence is contextual, not a universal emotion detector.

**Original contrast:** In C, G–C can close a phrase while a G ending can prepare continuation. Weaker evidence: Calling any last chord “tonic” solely because it is last confuses role with position.

**Five-minute task:** Make two endings for one saved sketch: finish on G, then G–C; explain the intended difference.

**Readiness / retrieval:** music-09, music-11. Review the named skills in the task; return to this sketch after a delay with one changed constraint.

**Assess:** Identifies key and chord function; compares a chosen phrase ending. The learner documents one choice, a comparison and an intended effect.

**Source support:** KHcad, J13. Task, comparison and rubric D.

### music-13 — Minor choices

**Objective / principle:** Compose in natural minor and compare a raised leading tone. Minor is not a compulsory sadness label.

**Original contrast:** E major contains G-sharp; Em contains G natural. The altered note changes the approach to A. Weaker evidence: Claiming E major is entirely inside A natural minor misses G-sharp.

**Five-minute task:** Make a short Am-centred ending; compare G–Am with E–Am and identify G versus G-sharp.

**Readiness / retrieval:** music-08, music-10, music-12. Review the named skills in the task; return to this sketch after a delay with one changed constraint.

**Assess:** Correct pitch collection/alteration; choice is linked to the intended effect. The learner documents one choice, a comparison and an intended effect.

**Source support:** Rminor, J2; exact task D. Task, comparison and rubric D.

### music-14 — When the harmony moves

**Objective / principle:** Change harmonic rhythm while keeping chord order fixed.

**Original contrast:** Holding C for a full bar versus two beats changes pace without changing the chord name. Weaker evidence: Adding more chords when asked to compare durations changes two variables at once.

**Five-minute task:** Try C–F–G–C with two different duration plans; document correctly counted bars.

**Readiness / retrieval:** music-03, music-11, music-12. Review the named skills in the task; return to this sketch after a delay with one changed constraint.

**Assess:** Chord-change times are clear; the explanation isolates harmonic rhythm. The learner documents one choice, a comparison and an intended effect.

**Source support:** J16. Task, comparison and rubric D.

### music-15 — Strumming as arrangement

**Objective / principle:** Use accent, omission and sustain to support an original phrase. Technique speed is not the goal.

**Original contrast:** Leaving beat 3 silent can make space around an arriving lyric or melody. Weaker evidence: Demonstrating a fast stock strum without connecting it to the phrase is weak composition evidence.

**Five-minute task:** Keep two accessible chords; make two attack grids differing at one accent or silence.

**Readiness / retrieval:** music-14. Review the named skills in the task; return to this sketch after a delay with one changed constraint.

**Assess:** Patterns are countable; the selected change serves a musical intention. The learner documents one choice, a comparison and an intended effect.

**Source support:** R6g, KH1. Task, comparison and rubric D.

### music-16 — Picking as texture

**Objective / principle:** Separate bass and upper notes to create a deliberate accompaniment.

**Original contrast:** With Am, an A bass followed by selected C/E notes creates a separable texture. Weaker evidence: An intricate pattern that obscures the intended melody does not meet a clarity goal merely by being difficult.

**Five-minute task:** Over one accessible chord, invent a one-bar bass/upper-note pattern and mark the attacks.

**Readiness / retrieval:** music-03, music-10. Review the named skills in the task; return to this sketch after a delay with one changed constraint.

**Assess:** Notes fit the intended sonority; bass and upper attacks have clear timing. The learner documents one choice, a comparison and an intended effect.

**Source support:** R6g. Task, comparison and rubric D.

### music-17 — Syncopation and groove

**Objective / principle:** Place an accent against the expected beat while keeping the underlying pulse clear.

**Original contrast:** An attack on 2-and sustained across beat 3 changes emphasis while the bar still totals four beats. Weaker evidence: Adding an extra eighth without removing time elsewhere overfills the bar.

**Five-minute task:** Shift one saved attack to an offbeat, leave one rest, and compare with the original.

**Readiness / retrieval:** music-03, music-15. Review the named skills in the task; return to this sketch after a delay with one changed constraint.

**Assess:** Count remains valid; the learner identifies the shifted accent and intended feel. The learner documents one choice, a comparison and an intended effect.

**Source support:** J3, KH1. Task, comparison and rubric D.

### music-18 — A groove for a phrase

**Objective / principle:** Combine accompaniment rhythm and harmonic rhythm in a short original section plan.

**Original contrast:** A sparse first bar and an extra attack before the arrival can create a purposeful contrast. Weaker evidence: A new difficult guitar pattern unrelated to the phrase does not demonstrate support.

**Five-minute task:** Revisit a saved two-bar sketch; change the accompaniment to support its phrase ending.

**Readiness / retrieval:** music-12, music-14, music-16, music-17. Review the named skills in the task; return to this sketch after a delay with one changed constraint.

**Assess:** Both rhythm layers are documented; at least two choices are justified. The learner documents one choice, a comparison and an intended effect.

**Source support:** KH1, R6g; stage challenge D. Task, comparison and rubric D.

## Stage 3: Melodic development and musical meaning

### music-19 — A motif worth keeping

**Objective / principle:** Identify a small pitch/rhythm identity that can survive development.

**Original contrast:** A short–short–long duration pattern can remain recognisable as pitches change. Weaker evidence: “These notes sound good” identifies no feature to develop.

**Five-minute task:** Compose a three-note motif, then name the feature another phrase must retain.

**Readiness / retrieval:** music-07, music-09. Review the named skills in the task; return to this sketch after a delay with one changed constraint.

**Assess:** Motif is reproducible from the plan; identity is specified. The learner documents one choice, a comparison and an intended effect.

**Source support:** KM18, J11. Task, comparison and rubric D.

### music-20 — Develop, do not just replace

**Objective / principle:** Change pitch shape while retaining rhythm, or change rhythm while retaining a pitch feature.

**Original contrast:** Retain two eighths plus a quarter while moving the pitch cell upward. Weaker evidence: Replacing all pitches, durations and phrase length gives little evidence of a related variant.

**Five-minute task:** Make one controlled variant of the motif and label what changed and stayed.

**Readiness / retrieval:** music-19. Review the named skills in the task; return to this sketch after a delay with one changed constraint.

**Assess:** At least one identifying feature is retained; one transformation is explained. The learner documents one choice, a comparison and an intended effect.

**Source support:** KM18, J11. Task, comparison and rubric D.

### music-21 — Contour and a destination

**Objective / principle:** Place an intentional melodic high point and distinguish exact from diatonic sequence.

**Original contrast:** A C–D–E cell shifted diatonically to D–E–F preserves step direction but changes one interval quality. Weaker evidence: Calling those two cells an exact semitone-preserving transposition is inaccurate.

**Five-minute task:** Shape a four-note outline with a chosen peak. Optional longer step: sequence one small cell within the chosen scale.

**Readiness / retrieval:** music-08, music-20. Review the named skills in the task; return to this sketch after a delay with one changed constraint.

**Assess:** Peak placement has a purpose; sequence type is described correctly. The learner documents one choice, a comparison and an intended effect.

**Source support:** J7, J11; optional sequencing extension may take longer. Task, comparison and rubric D.

### music-22 — Phrases need air

**Objective / principle:** Use a rest, held note or response to shape a phrase boundary.

**Original contrast:** A one-beat rest after the first idea can separate it from the answer. Weaker evidence: A continuous note list with no duration or separation does not document phrase space.

**Five-minute task:** Make two short answering phrases with a documented rest; play/hum them and note a breathing observation.

**Readiness / retrieval:** music-14, music-21. Review the named skills in the task; return to this sketch after a delay with one changed constraint.

**Assess:** Boundaries and rests are clear; reflection relates space to the intention. The learner documents one choice, a comparison and an intended effect.

**Source support:** J1, KM2. Task, comparison and rubric D.

### music-23 — Melody meets harmony

**Objective / principle:** Distinguish chord tones from stepwise connections in an original phrase.

**Original contrast:** C–D–E over C can place a non-chord D between chord tones; placement affects the result. Weaker evidence: Calling every scale note a chord tone ignores the actual chord.

**Five-minute task:** Over C, mark an arrival on E and a chosen connecting D; document timing and then revise one note.

**Readiness / retrieval:** music-10, music-22. Review the named skills in the task; return to this sketch after a delay with one changed constraint.

**Assess:** Correctly labels notes relative to the chord; compares an intended connection. The learner documents one choice, a comparison and an intended effect.

**Source support:** J7, J16. Task, comparison and rubric D.

### music-24 — One melody, two harmonies

**Objective / principle:** Keep a melody note fixed and compare harmonic alternatives.

**Original contrast:** E is C’s major third and Am’s fifth; the same note has a different harmonic role. Weaker evidence: Moving the melody and chord simultaneously cannot isolate the harmonic effect.

**Five-minute task:** Try a sustained E over C and Am; spell each chord and justify one setting.

**Readiness / retrieval:** music-23. Review the named skills in the task; return to this sketch after a delay with one changed constraint.

**Assess:** Shared pitch is preserved; explanation identifies its two roles. The learner documents one choice, a comparison and an intended effect.

**Source support:** J16, KH36. Task, comparison and rubric D.

### music-25 — Delay the release

**Objective / principle:** Create an intentional tension and choose whether/when to resolve it.

**Original contrast:** The F can resolve by semitone to E; delaying it changes the expectation. Weaker evidence: Claiming every F is wrong in C treats an intentional non-chord tone as an automatic error.

**Five-minute task:** Over C, compare a short F–E gesture with an F held longer; state the context and intended release.

**Readiness / retrieval:** music-12, music-23. Review the named skills in the task; return to this sketch after a delay with one changed constraint.

**Assess:** Describes the dissonance in context; controls the selected resolution timing. The learner documents one choice, a comparison and an intended effect.

**Source support:** J2, J4. Task, comparison and rubric D.

### music-26 — A developed sketch

**Objective / principle:** Combine motif development, contour and a harmonic choice.

**Original contrast:** The same rhythmic cell returns with a new peak over a deliberately chosen chord. Weaker evidence: Repeating a chord exercise without a developing motif misses this integration task.

**Five-minute task:** Extend an existing sketch by one bar; retain motif identity and explain one harmonic arrival.

**Readiness / retrieval:** music-20, music-21, music-24, music-25. Review the named skills in the task; return to this sketch after a delay with one changed constraint.

**Assess:** At least two earlier skills are evident; one revision follows the comparison. The learner documents one choice, a comparison and an intended effect.

**Source support:** J11, J16, KM24; stage challenge D. Task, comparison and rubric D.

### music-27 — Fit the voice: transpose and capo

**Objective / principle:** Preserve musical relationships while changing pitch level for a comfortable range.

**Original contrast:** C/F/G shapes at capo 2 sound D/G/A; chord labels must distinguish shape from sound. Weaker evidence: Labeling capo-2 C shape as sounding C creates a wrong plan.

**Five-minute task:** Move an existing C–F–G sketch up two semitones with a capo or changed shapes; label sounding chords.

**Readiness / retrieval:** music-06, music-11, music-22. Review the named skills in the task; return to this sketch after a delay with one changed constraint.

**Assess:** All pitches move consistently; the learner reports a comfortable trial without AI claiming to hear it. The learner documents one choice, a comparison and an intended effect.

**Source support:** KHbasics, Rcapo. Task, comparison and rubric D.

## Stage 4: Complete acoustic composition

### music-28 — A returning musical hook

**Objective / principle:** Make an identifiable return whose variation has a purpose.

**Original contrast:** An unchanged opening rhythm followed by a different final note can mark a new context. Weaker evidence: Repetition quantity alone cannot demonstrate memorability to an audience.

**Five-minute task:** Repeat a short saved motif, then vary its ending; describe what makes the return recognisable.

**Readiness / retrieval:** music-19, music-22. Review the named skills in the task; return to this sketch after a delay with one changed constraint.

**Assess:** Identity survives the variation; intended effect is justified. The learner documents one choice, a comparison and an intended effect.

**Source support:** KM19, KM1; audience memorability remains untested. Task, comparison and rubric D.

### music-29 — Verse and chorus contrast

**Objective / principle:** Give sections different compositional jobs.

**Original contrast:** A close-range conversational verse can lead to a more spacious chorus; the contrast must fit this song. Weaker evidence: Renaming the same unaltered section “chorus” supplies no contrast evidence.

**Five-minute task:** Plan two sections and revise one two-bar excerpt by changing register, density or harmonic rhythm.

**Readiness / retrieval:** music-18, music-26, music-28. Review the named skills in the task; return to this sketch after a delay with one changed constraint.

**Assess:** A concrete changed feature is identified; the return remains coherent. The learner documents one choice, a comparison and an intended effect.

**Source support:** KM24, S8. Task, comparison and rubric D.

### music-30 — A bridge and a route back

**Objective / principle:** Create purposeful contrast and prepare a return.

**Original contrast:** An ending on G can prepare a C-centred chorus after a contrasting section. Weaker evidence: A bridge inserted solely because a template requires one has no stated structural job.

**Five-minute task:** Sketch a two-bar bridge ending and its route to the chorus; explain why the extra section belongs.

**Readiness / retrieval:** music-12, music-29. Review the named skills in the task; return to this sketch after a delay with one changed constraint.

**Assess:** Contrast adds a role; the harmonic/melodic route back is documented. The learner documents one choice, a comparison and an intended effect.

**Source support:** KHcad, R1; specific bridge task D. Task, comparison and rubric D.

### music-31 — Substitute with a reason

**Objective / principle:** Use a shared tone or planned altered colour to revise harmony.

**Original contrast:** A retained E can remain a chord tone under either choice. Weaker evidence: Calling chords interchangeable in every context because they share two notes overgeneralises.

**Five-minute task:** Keep an original melody fixed; replace one C chord with Am and compare the documented roles.

**Readiness / retrieval:** music-24, music-25. Review the named skills in the task; return to this sketch after a delay with one changed constraint.

**Assess:** Spells both chords correctly; checks the melody and intended contextual effect. The learner documents one choice, a comparison and an intended effect.

**Source support:** KH32, J16. Task, comparison and rubric D.

### music-32 — Sevenths and colour

**Objective / principle:** Construct and select a seventh sonority to support an original phrase.

**Original contrast:** Cmaj7 adds B; C7 adds B-flat. Those are different sonorities. Weaker evidence: Treating C7 as shorthand for Cmaj7 is a naming error.

**Five-minute task:** Compare C with Cmaj7 under a short saved idea. Identify B versus B-flat in the supplied original C7 diagram; trying the third sonority is an optional extension.

**Readiness / retrieval:** music-10, music-31. Review the named skills in the task; return to this sketch after a delay with one changed constraint.

**Assess:** Correct notes/labels; an intentional comparison rather than automatic “richer is better”. The learner documents one choice, a comparison and an intended effect.

**Source support:** KHbasics, KMref; three-way task can extend beyond five minutes. Task, comparison and rubric D.

### music-33 — Bass is a compositional voice

**Objective / principle:** Separate chord identity from its bass and consider smooth movement.

**Original contrast:** C/E still contains C–E–G but places E below; a stepwise bass plan may connect voicings. Weaker evidence: Changing the bass to a non-chord note and calling it a first inversion is inaccurate.

**Five-minute task:** Keep a C chord’s notes, compare C with E as the lowest note, and write one short bass connection.

**Readiness / retrieval:** music-10, music-24, music-31. Review the named skills in the task; return to this sketch after a delay with one changed constraint.

**Assess:** Bass/chord tones agree with labels; movement is chosen for the phrase. The learner documents one choice, a comparison and an intended effect.

**Source support:** R11, J16; inversion/voice-leading scaffold D. Task, comparison and rubric D.

### music-34 — Arrange for one acoustic guitar

**Objective / principle:** Balance bass, harmony and melodic/lyric space using a playable simplification.

**Original contrast:** A sparse bass-and-upper-note texture can leave room for the sung phrase. Weaker evidence: Trying to play every melody note and full strum at once may defeat a deliberately clear accompaniment.

**Five-minute task:** Arrange one bar of saved material; remove one competing attack and note a reach/comfort observation.

**Readiness / retrieval:** music-16, music-27, music-29, music-33. Review the named skills in the task; return to this sketch after a delay with one changed constraint.

**Assess:** Parts have distinguishable roles; simplification serves intent rather than speed. The learner documents one choice, a comparison and an intended effect.

**Source support:** R6g, R11, J1; physical comfort self-reported. Task, comparison and rubric D.

### music-35 — A whole form from existing pieces

**Objective / principle:** Assemble an original section plan with recurrence, contrast and a considered ending.

**Original contrast:** A return of the hook after a changed bridge can carry a purposeful final variation. Weaker evidence: Appending unrelated sketches without transition or recurring identity gives weak form evidence.

**Five-minute task:** Place saved sections in a form map; revise one transition. Finish over optional longer sessions.

**Readiness / retrieval:** music-28, music-29, music-30, music-34. Review the named skills in the task; return to this sketch after a delay with one changed constraint.

**Assess:** Form has recurring/developing material; transition and ending choices are explained. The learner documents one choice, a comparison and an intended effect.

**Source support:** KM24, P18, R1; assembly design D. Task, comparison and rubric D.

### music-36 — Independent composition portfolio

**Objective / principle:** Revise an acoustic miniature and test transfer to a new brief.

**Original contrast:** Defend a melodic change, a harmonic choice and an accompaniment simplification with before/after evidence. Weaker evidence: A completed export or high XP total is not evidence of independent composition.

**Five-minute task:** Revise one passage of the assembled work, then plan a new two-bar response under an unfamiliar constraint.

**Readiness / retrieval:** music-26, music-32, music-35. Review the named skills in the task; return to this sketch after a delay with one changed constraint.

**Assess:** Specific revisions support the intention; later unfamiliar work shows transfer or identifies a practice need. The learner documents one choice, a comparison and an intended effect.

**Source support:** D; conceptual integration from J1, J16, R11, KM24. Task, comparison and rubric D.

A conceptual check verifies that a note belongs to a chord or a bar adds up; an artistic judgment weighs the stated intention and alternatives. Passing the conceptual check alone does not establish a compelling song. Learners listen/play for themselves and record what they noticed. Phase 2 local audio capture/playback preserves ideas without sending recordings to AI; actual machine evaluation still requires a separate reviewed design and is not implemented.

## Review and optional depth

After 9, use a different subject and rhythm; after 18, preserve the motif but change the accompaniment; after 26, reharmonise a saved phrase then make a fresh response; after 36, revisit an earlier sketch and compare transfer after a delay. Shorten a motif or sequence it as a later variation, not a new completion badge for every transformation. Optional longer work explores modes, modulation, altered tunings and more developed counterpoint only after a learner needs them. These topics are in the library, but their entire example sets have not been verified; they are not represented as finished advanced modules in this release.

Five-minute timing for units 21 and 32 should be trialled: the minimum task is one comparison, with the second variation labelled an extension. Independence grows through practice beyond these 36 introductions; this is a composition foundation through advanced integration, not a guarantee of advanced musicianship in three hours.
